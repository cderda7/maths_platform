Assessment for the Edexia product and pedagogy teams

The headline: you've built the best classroom loop I've seen for making students' mathematical thinking visible and acting on it within the same lesson. The problems are a handful of design choices that work against your own principles, plus a trust risk in the AI suggestions.

What's genuinely excellent

Holistic Assessment is the standout feature. "Minus signs wrong on four sets: in a surd bracket, factor pairs, turning points, concavity and completing the square" is a diagnosis most teachers would never assemble themselves. It spots one underlying habit showing up across different topics. You can click from that summary through a table of every set to the exact line in Problem Set 4 Q1, shown next to the group's rework. That's what you should lead with.

Class review follows a sound "I do, you do" structure. The board reveals a matched worked example (2x²+11x−6) line by line, then "Everyone writes this one on their own iPad" with a fresh variant (2x²+7x−15), marked as each line is written. Before that, the A/B/C comparison shows anonymised real answers, with "your approach" tagged only on your own iPad.

Refine is a smart idea. "Q9 is Q3 again with the numbers changed… some spacing before they meet the type again will do more than a repeat" and "Nothing in the set is set in a context" are the comments a good head of maths makes when looking over a colleague's problem set. Grounding a suggestion in class data ("7 of 20 students slipped on the sign of the factor pair") is exactly right.

Personalised homework is well built. It opens with "From your mistakes", grouped by the set each mistake came from and using fresh variants rather than the same numbers, then an "Everyone" section. That's spaced practice on each student's real errors.

"I need help" gives a matched example, not a hint. Stuck on x²−5x+6? You get a worked x²−9x+14, then a completion problem, then you go back to Q1.

Review stages are configurable, including "working only". This answers my earlier worry about lesson time, and I withdraw it.

What I'd change, in priority order
Take the medals off the board. The race bars that reorder and the gold/silver/bronze on the projector are the single biggest contradiction in the product. Every other screen says mistakes are normal and discussion matters, and this one rewards whichever group finishes fastest. It also nudges the strongest student to take the pen. Show the teacher who's finished, privately.
Audit the AI's suggestions before teachers learn to rubber-stamp them. I found two problems in the first two sets I built.
It said "every factorisation in this set uses two positive numbers" when Q2 (8 and −1) and Q5 (−5 and 1) don't. The suggested change was still sensible, but the reason given was false, and teachers will notice.
It recommended changing y=(x−3)²+4 because "a student working alone at home who finds no x-intercepts can't tell a right answer from a slip". I'd push back hard. A question with no x-intercepts is exactly the kind students need to meet, since it forces them to check whether their answer makes sense. The fix is to make the answer checkable (a "does it cross the axis? how do you know?" prompt), not to delete the question. The Refine feature needs a pedagogy review of its own heuristics.
Smaller label problems belong here too. Problem Set 6 Q6 is tagged "complex unfamiliar" but is a textbook discriminant question. Homework Q8 is tagged "simple familiar" but is the no-intercepts trap. In Problem Set 6 Q3, x²+x−6=6 is tagged "sign lost collecting terms" when the slip is in the expansion.
Show results live during "Students' turn" in class review. The teacher's screen just says "The class is writing" while every student writes a fully marked answer on their iPad. That's the moment the teacher most needs to know how many got it right and where the others went wrong. Without it, "Next question" is a guess.
Replace the student self-diagnosis steps. The warm-up asks "tell me about your concerns with factorising", and "I need help" asks "Which skill?". Both ask students to name gaps they often can't see, and in the warm-up the canned replies misfired: I said fractions were fine, it replied "that's a tricky skill" and started with fractions. Use one or two quick diagnostic questions, or infer the skill from the line where the student stopped.
Give the waiting screen something to do. "Waiting for the class, 2 of 19 handed in" is dead time for your fastest students, several minutes of it. Offer an extension question, a "find the error" task, or a question to predict ahead of group review.
Separate symptoms from misconceptions in the labels. "Brackets don't expand back" is a check the student skipped, and it's the top gap on four sets. That tells the teacher students aren't checking, not why their factorising is wrong. Pair each symptom label with a likely cause.
Recalibrate "secure". Sam's reasoning was "secure" on Problem Sets 1 to 5 and a "gap" on Set 6, which shows the label doesn't mean secure. Liam shows 0/10 on Problem Set 4 but "secure" in Communication and "confident". Only award secure after success on a later occasion, and don't award it with no evidence at all.
Check the worked examples. Add a brief "why" line to each step. In the fractions example, clear denominators by multiplying through rather than converting 6 to 12/2. Vary the surface form across example, "your turn" and "on your own", so students have to think rather than copy the pattern.
Watch homework length and ask whether students would actually do it. Sam's Homework 3 has about eight questions from his mistakes plus ten for everyone. Also, the "Missing: nothing missing" list sits beside an "HW2 missing" tile, which is the kind of inconsistency that erodes trust in a live product.
Decide how you'll handle access. Needing an iPad and pen per student will rule out most state schools. Consider a shared device per group, or paper working captured by photo, even if the richness drops.
What Eedi does better

Eedi's strength is exactly where Edexia is weakest.

Misconception labels you can trust. Each wrong answer maps to a misconception tested against very large numbers of real responses. That makes it comparable across schools and much less exposed to the kind of AI mislabelling I found in Refine.
Speed and low friction. A diagnostic question takes seconds, fits a starter or an exit ticket, and works on any device. Edexia's loop at its best needs a full lesson and serious hardware.
Guaranteed item quality. Eedi's questions are carefully designed and fixed. Edexia's quality depends on whatever the teacher uploads plus AI edits that can be wrong.
What I'd take back to Eedi
Holistic Assessment-style summaries. Eedi has the misconception data to write "this student keeps making the same sign error across topics", which would be more useful than a topic-by-topic list of gaps.
Worked example, then a matched question, with results visible to the teacher live. Eedi could run this across a whole class on its existing question format, and include the live results that Edexia misses.
Critiquing a teacher's own question set. Flagging near-duplicates, missing context questions and untested misconceptions, grounded in class data, would suit Eedi well. Eedi's validated misconception data would keep the suggestions more reliable than what I saw here.
Anonymised A/B/C answer comparison with a private "this was yours". A strong in-lesson routine built from real student answers.
Homework built from each student's past mistakes, using fresh variants. Eedi's data could drive this more reliably.
Seeing the student's working. An optional "show your working" on selected questions would turn a likely misconception into a confirmed one.
